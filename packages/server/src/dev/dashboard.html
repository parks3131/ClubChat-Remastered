How to read this

Everything here is emitted by the server, so it sees what the client actually sent rather than what the client meant to send.

There are two wires, not one

REST on :3000 carries every read and every command except a chat send. The socket on :3001 carries the sends, the acks and the live fan-out. Tapping Send in a chat makes zero REST calls, which is why a browser network panel alone makes chat look inert.

A tap does not end at the response

A command writes its domain rows and an outbox row in one transaction. The worker drains that outbox and runs the effect: the system message, the notification rows, the push. Those are the EFFECT lines, and they are the part no client-side tool can see.

Grouping is by time, not by a trace id

A REST call or an inbound frame opens a group; sockets frames and effects that follow within 2.5s are drawn underneath it. That is an inference, and it is right when one person is clicking one thing at a time. With two clients busy at once, read the timestamps rather than the indentation.

Coverage

The left column is the server's own route table. A route lights up the first time you exercise it, so "click every button" has an end. Click a route to read what SPEC/TECH/10-protocol.md says about it and to filter the timeline to it.

What is deliberately missing

Anything under a key matching pass, secret, token, authorization or signature is replaced before it leaves the server, at any depth. Long strings and long arrays are cut. So a sign-in shows its shape and never its password.
